The target `77` has two symbols. The audio may produce dozens of output frames. We do not know which frame should carry each seven, and we do not label those timestamps by hand.

CTC trains by accounting for multiple frame paths that collapse to the same target. Greedy decoding later chooses one path; training and decoding are related but different operations.

## Collapse Rules

<STTLab mode="ctc" client:load />

First merge adjacent identical frame labels, then remove blank. `7 7 _ 7` becomes `77`. If you remove blank before merging, the two sevens become neighbours and you incorrectly return `7`.

## Training Objective

The model gives a distribution at each frame. Valid paths allocate characters and blanks differently while preserving target order. The loss accounts for their combined probability rather than forcing one hand-picked alignment. The [CTC visual reference](https://distill.pub/2017/ctc/) develops this distinction.

```python
criterion = torch.nn.CTCLoss(blank=0, zero_infinity=False)
loss = criterion(log_probs, targets, output_lengths, target_lengths)
```

Our tensor order for log probabilities is time × batch × vocabulary. Targets contain no blank tokens. [The PyTorch reference](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/generated/torch.nn.CTCLoss.html) documents the accepted shapes.

## Failure Signals

An impossible alignment can produce an infinite loss. Do not immediately hide that with `zero_infinity=True`. Inspect time downsampling, repeated characters and target lengths first. A zeroed loss can make a broken example stop contributing while the overall run appears healthy.
