In the recorded CPU comparison, HiFi-GAN took 96.4% of synthesis time. The acoustic model took the rest. Inspect the waveform stage before trying to make the text encoder faster.

## Waveform Synthesis

A neural vocoder is the network that turns a mel spectrogram into a waveform. The acoustic model says how much energy each frequency band carries in each frame; the vocoder produces the samples.

That is a large expansion. Our vocoder takes 80 numbers per frame and emits 256 audio samples for each one, at 22,050 samples a second. And the 80 numbers are incomplete: a mel spectrogram keeps magnitude and throws away phase, the timing relationship between frequency components. [Why TTS needs a vocoder](/learn/100x/tts/why-tts-needs-a-vocoder) shows what is lost. The vocoder has to generate a waveform whose spectrum matches the mel, with phase that holds together from one frame to the next.

Classical signal processing can estimate phase iteratively. Neural vocoders learn it from data instead, which is why they need training on a lot of audio and why they are reused so widely.

## HiFi-GAN Architecture

HiFi-GAN is a generative adversarial network: a generator that upsamples mel frames into a waveform, trained against discriminators that try to tell its output from real audio.

NVIDIA's [NeMo documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/tts/models.html) summarises the design as "one generator and two discriminators (multi-scale and multi-period)". The generator upsamples with transposed convolutions, each followed by a multi-receptive field fusion module that looks at the signal at several scales at once. The multi-period discriminator looks at the audio folded at several fixed periods, which matches the [HiFi-GAN paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05646)'s central argument: speech is made of periodic signals, so modelling periodic patterns is what lifts sample quality.

The paper reports generating 22.05 kHz audio 168 times faster than real time on a V100 GPU, and a lightweight variant at 13.4 times faster than real time on CPU. Those are the authors' numbers on their hardware. Ours follow.

## Vocoder Timing

On our container, the vocoder alone ran at a real-time factor of 0.19 to 0.27 on a 7.55-second LJSpeech clip, so roughly 4 to 5 times faster than real time. The 0.27 was the first run, with cold caches.

Real-time factor is synthesis time divided by audio duration: below 1 is faster than real time. We timed the two stages separately in `compare_piper`, our Piper comparison run, which is the source of the 3.6% and 96.4% split. The timing brackets the vocoder call like this:

```python
m = model.infer(ids)[0] * c["stats"]["mel_std"] + c["stats"]["mel_mean"]
t1 = time.time()
wav = voc.decode_batch(m.T.unsqueeze(0)).squeeze().numpy()
t2 = time.time()
```

The first row comes from four vocoder runs on that one clip: copy synthesis plus the three mismatch variants from the next lesson. The other two rows are three probe sentences, two timed passes each after a warm-up pass:

| System, 4 CPU cores                | Real-time factor | Share of synthesis time          |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------- |
| HiFi-GAN alone, one 7.55 s clip    | 0.19 to 0.27     | all of it                        |
| Tiny model (step 1,650) + HiFi-GAN | 0.24             | acoustic 3.6%, vocoder 96.4%     |
| Piper, VITS end-to-end             | 0.052            | one network, no separate vocoder |

## Pretrained Weights

Because a compatible public HiFi-GAN checkpoint already exists, letting this course focus its training work on the acoustic model: copy synthesis through it lands 12.3 dB from the original by log-spectral distance. We used [speechbrain/tts-hifigan-ljspeech](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/tts-hifigan-ljspeech), Apache-2.0, trained on the same dataset.

The test that it fits is copy synthesis: take a real clip, compute its mel with the vocoder's own recipe, and vocode it. No acoustic model is involved, so the result isolates waveform reconstruction. It is a reference, not a strict bound on every predicted mel or listening score. On LJ001-0009, copy synthesis came out at a log-spectral distance of 12.3 dB from the original. The catch is the recipe. The vocoder expects mels computed the way it was trained, and some deviations matter far more than others. Its recipe: 22,050 Hz, n_fft 1024, hop 256, 80 bins from 0 to 8,000 Hz, Slaney mel scale and norm, natural-log compression. Our tiny model exists to predict exactly that. [Mel recipe mismatch](/learn/100x/tts/mel-recipe-mismatch) measures what happens when one setting is off.
