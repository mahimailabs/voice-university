The [VALL-E paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02111) reports synthesising an unseen speaker from "a 3-second enrolled recording" used as a prompt, after training on 60K hours of English speech. Our own model, trained on 0.74 h of one speaker, cannot imitate anyone. The gap is partly scale and partly representation: VALL-E does not predict a mel spectrogram at all.

By the end of this page you will know what a neural audio codec produces, why turning speech into tokens made few-second cloning possible, and what NVIDIA's Magpie-TTS adds to stop a token model from wandering off the script. None of this was trained by us; it is sourced description, placed next to the cascade you built in units 4 and 5.

## What is a neural audio codec?

A neural audio codec is an encoder and decoder trained together to compress a waveform into a short sequence of discrete codes and rebuild it. The codes are integers from a fixed codebook, which is exactly what a language-model-style network knows how to predict.

[SoundStream](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03312) is the reference design. Its encoder turns audio into latent frames, a residual vector quantiser (RVQ) maps each frame to codebook entries, and a decoder turns the codes back into audio. RVQ works in layers: the first codebook captures a coarse approximation, and each later codebook encodes what the previous ones missed. The paper reports bitrates from 3 kbps to 18 kbps from one model, and running "in real time on a smartphone CPU". EnCodec, from Meta, follows the same shape: a streaming encoder-decoder with a quantised latent space.

Compare this to the mel recipe from unit 1. A mel spectrogram is continuous, throws away phase, and needs a separately trained vocoder whose recipe must match exactly ([the mismatch lesson](/learn/100x/tts/mel-recipe-mismatch) measured what happens when it does not). A codec token sequence is discrete, and the decoder that turns it into audio was trained with the encoder, so the pair agree by construction.

## How does a codec-token TTS model generate speech?

It treats synthesis as sequence prediction. Text goes in, the model predicts codec tokens one step at a time, and the codec decoder produces the waveform.

VALL-E framed TTS as "a conditional language modeling task rather than continuous signal regression." That framing is what makes prompt-based cloning cheap. Encode a few seconds of a new speaker with the same codec, put those tokens in front of the sequence, and the model continues in the voice it was shown. The paper also notes that the prompt's emotion and acoustic environment carry through, which cuts both ways: a noisy reference produces noisy output.

The price is the failure mode. An autoregressive token model has no duration predictor and no length regulator. Our model from [training from scratch](/learn/100x/tts/train-tts-model-from-scratch) cannot skip a symbol it was given, because every character gets an explicit number of frames. (Digits it was never given are another matter: they are dropped before the model runs.) A token model decides for itself when to move on, so it can repeat, skip or keep talking past the end of the text. That is the subject of [TTS hallucinations](/learn/100x/tts/tts-hallucinations).

## What does Magpie-TTS add?

Magpie-TTS is NVIDIA's codec-token model in NeMo. The [Magpie-TTS documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/tts/magpietts.html) describes "an encoder-decoder transformer architecture that operates on discrete audio tokens produced by a neural audio codec." The encoder reads the text; the decoder generates audio tokens autoregressively while attending to the text and to any audio context.

Four pieces from the docs are worth knowing:

- **Monotonic alignment.** A CTC loss and an attention prior bias the decoder's cross-attention towards moving forward through the text. This is the direct answer to skips and repeats.
- **Classifier-free guidance.** During training the model sometimes drops its conditioning. At inference the difference between conditioned and unconditioned outputs is amplified, which the docs say increases fidelity and consistency.
- **Context encoder for cloning.** Reference audio goes through a dedicated context encoder, so the model can speak in a voice from "just a few seconds of reference audio". Text descriptions can also condition speaking style.
- **Frame stacking.** A base decoder predicts one latent per group of frames and a small local transformer expands it, with a stacking factor up to 4. Fewer big decoder steps means faster inference.

The docs list a 357M-parameter multilingual checkpoint. For scale, our tiny model has 1,231,185 parameters; Magpie is a different class of system, built for GPUs.

## How does preference optimisation improve TTS?

Preference optimisation fine-tunes a trained model towards outputs that score better on measurable rewards. NeMo's [Magpie-TTS preference optimisation page](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/tts/magpietts-po.html) describes two routes, and both use the same rewards: character error rate from an ASR model for intelligibility, speaker similarity for voice consistency, and optionally PESQ for audio quality.

**DPO** is offline. Generate several samples per text and reference pair (the docs default to 6), keep the best and worst by the metrics, and fine-tune on those chosen and rejected pairs. Recommended settings on the page are a DPO beta of 0.01 and a learning rate of 2e-7.

**GRPO** is online. It generates candidates during training (12 per example in the recommended settings, at temperature 0.7 to 0.8), scores them, and pushes the model towards higher reward, with a learning rate of 1e-7. The docs call it "generally recommended over DPO", partly because nothing needs to be pre-generated and stored.

Notice the reward. CER from an ASR model is the same idea as the output WER we compute in the [eval harness](/learn/100x/tts/tts-eval-harness): transcribe the synthesis and compare it to the input text. The difference is where it sits. We use it to judge a model; Magpie's preference stage uses it to train one.
