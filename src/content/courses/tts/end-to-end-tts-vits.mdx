Piper pairs a VITS voice with ONNX Runtime and a phoneme front end. Our experiment pairs a small acoustic model with HiFi-GAN. Compare their audio and timings, without treating the gap as an architecture-only result.

## VITS Architecture

An end-to-end model turns text symbols into a waveform in one network. NeMo's [TTS overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/tts/intro.html) puts it as integrating the text, acoustic and vocoder stages "as a single model so that it directly synthesizes audios from graphemes/phonemes inputs without any intermediate processes."

[VITS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06103) is the model most people mean. Its paper combines three ideas:

- a **variational autoencoder**, whose latent space stands in for the mel spectrogram and is never handed to a separate model;
- **normalising flows**, which make the text-conditioned prior over that latent more expressive;
- **adversarial training**, with a waveform decoder trained the way a GAN vocoder is.

It also replaces a deterministic duration predictor with a stochastic one, so the same sentence can come out with different rhythms. The authors report that on LJSpeech it reaches a mean opinion score comparable to the ground-truth recordings. We did not measure MOS for anything in this build.

[Piper](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl) packages VITS voices for local use, exported to ONNX, with espeak-ng for phonemisation in front. Its LJSpeech voice was trained by its author for 1,000 epochs at "medium" quality, according to the voice card.

## Piper Comparison

On three probe sentences, two timed passes each after a warm-up pass, transcribed by Whisper base.en:

| 4 CPU cores, no GPU | Our cascade (step 1,650 + HiFi-GAN)         | Piper en_US-ljspeech-medium                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Mean output WER     | 21%                                         | 1.9%                                        |
| Real-time factor    | 0.24 (about 4x faster than real time)       | 0.052 (about 19x)                           |
| Size                | 1,231,185 params + pretrained vocoder       | 63.5 MB ONNX file                           |
| Training            | 1,650 steps (200 s), 0.74 h of audio, by us | 1,000 epochs on LJSpeech, by Piper's author |

Treat the WER column as indicative. Three sentences is 28 words, and Piper's 1.9% is a single substitution: on one of its two passes Whisper heard "A quick brown fox" for "The quick brown fox". Every other Piper transcript was exact. On the larger test in the [eval harness](/learn/100x/tts/tts-eval-harness), 45 held-out sentences and 821 words, the gap holds: Piper 4.0%, our best checkpoint 19.7%, against 1.7% for Whisper on the original recordings.

## Runtime Differences

We did not profile it, so this section is reasoning, clearly labelled. Our measurement only says the whole Piper pass took 0.052 of real time and our two-stage pass took 0.24, with 96.4% of ours in the HiFi-GAN vocoder.

Plausible contributors: Piper runs through ONNX Runtime while our pipeline runs eager PyTorch; its "medium" voice may use a smaller waveform decoder than the SpeechBrain HiFi-GAN; and it makes one pass instead of two. Any of these could dominate. If speed decides your choice, time both on your own hardware rather than trusting our explanation.

## Architecture Tradeoffs

The mel spectrogram as an interface, and everything that interface gave you.

- **No stage-by-stage debugging.** In a cascade, [copy synthesis](/learn/100x/tts/what-is-a-neural-vocoder) isolates the vocoder, and a bad mel points at the acoustic model. In VITS the two are one network.
- **No mixing and matching.** We paired a model trained for 200 seconds with a vocoder someone else trained. With VITS you train or fine-tune the whole thing; Piper's author trained this voice for 1,000 epochs.
- **No mel recipe bug.** The flip side: the failure measured in [mel recipe mismatch](/learn/100x/tts/mel-recipe-mismatch) cannot happen, because nothing hands a mel from one model to another.
- **Two licences to check.** The piper-tts runtime is GPL-3.0-or-later; the [piper-voices](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices) weights are MIT. [Dataset licensing](/learn/100x/tts/tts-dataset-licensing) covers why that split matters.

<Practice label="Cascade or end-to-end">
  Ship an end-to-end voice when a good one exists for your language and speaker and you want the
  fewest moving parts. Keep a cascade when you need to swap the vocoder, debug stages separately, or
  train an acoustic model on data too small for a full end-to-end run.
</Practice>
